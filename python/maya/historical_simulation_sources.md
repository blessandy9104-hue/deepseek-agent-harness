# Historical holdout simulation source notes

## Selected case: Apollo 13, April 1970

This is a documented engineering-crisis case with a clear information cutoff and a known outcome. The simulation will give Maya only the events up to and immediately following the onboard oxygen-tank failure, while withholding the later rescue sequence.

### Authoritative sources

1. NASA, **Apollo 13: Mission Details**: https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-13-mission-details/
2. NASA History Office, **Detailed Chronology of Events Surrounding the Apollo 13 Accident**: https://www.nasa.gov/history/detailed-chronology-of-events-surrounding-the-apollo-13-accident/

### Pre-cutoff facts to provide to Maya

- Apollo 13 was intended to land in the Fra Mauro area of the Moon.
- The mission had generally appeared smooth during the first two days.
- Ground tests had already revealed a problem with oxygen tank No. 2 during detanking; the tank had been damaged earlier and an electrical-heater procedure was used to boil off oxygen.
- At about 55 hours 53 minutes ground elapsed time, after the crew was instructed to stir the cryogenic tanks, tank No. 2 showed pressure and sensor abnormalities.
- An electrical disturbance and a large bang were followed by main-bus undervoltage, loss of fuel-cell capacity, and rapid loss of oxygen.
- The crew reported a problem, and the second oxygen tank was also losing pressure.
- The command module’s ordinary power, light, and water supply were threatened.

### Held-out outcome facts

- The lunar landing was aborted.
- The lunar module was used as a lifeboat while controllers developed new procedures.
- The crew conserved power, water, and carbon-dioxide removal capacity and returned safely to Earth.
- Apollo 13 later became known as a successful failure because the crew was rescued and substantial operational learning resulted.

### Evaluation rule

This is not a test of supernatural prediction. A strong result should identify the dominant system risks, rank plausible next states, explicitly state uncertainty, distinguish observed facts from inferred causes, and recommend reversible safety-preserving actions. It should not claim an exact outcome from sparse evidence.
